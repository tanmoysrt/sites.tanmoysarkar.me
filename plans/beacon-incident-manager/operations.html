Beacon operator review

A responder should see what failed, how current the evidence is, who is acting, and what to do next. The current forms hide too much of this context.

Reviewed 2026-11-13 · Current implementation reviewed; changes below are proposed.

Review method and limits

I read all 19 DocType definitions, their controllers and form scripts, the workflow objects, job entry points, public API and Vue views. I opened the local Desk forms and public routes. The five unused HTML panels and the UTC clock mismatch were checked directly. The invalid public incident route returned a safe message.

No incident was acknowledged, published, resolved or paged. No silence, source removal or maintenance action was submitted. Findings about these actions come from source tracing, including the Frappe document-method handler. Provider delivery, concurrency and scheduler recovery still need controlled verification. Browser screenshot calls timed out, so the live review used DOM inspection. Proposed mockups have separate desktop and phone visual checks.

P1 means a response or customer-status contract is broken. P2 means the flow should be corrected before routine operational use. These are priorities for this review, not a production incident severity.

Design rules

  • Answer four questions first: what is wrong, where, who is responding, and what should happen next.
  • Show current status and the time of the last successful measurement together. A recent page refresh does not prove fresh telemetry.
  • Keep evidence and frequent actions on the first screen. Put credentials, SQL, identifiers and bookkeeping behind later tabs or collapsed sections.
  • Use one primary action for the current state. Keep deliberate confirmations for publication, suppression, destructive changes and paid phone calls.
  • Use native Desk forms, lists, Connections and document methods. Do not add a second operations frontend or a generic internal API.
  • Treat Unknown, suppressed, disabled and recovered as different conditions. Preserve the human decision to publish.

The approach follows Google's guidance on a working incident record, actionable monitoring and timely measurements. It keeps Beacon small: no new workflow engine, internal API layer, frontend framework or stored status just for display. See Incident response, Monitoring distributed systems, and Monitoring.

P1 · F01 The UTC requirement is not enforced

Current behavior. The reviewed local site uses a non-UTC time zone. Its naive clock differs from UTC by 5 hours 30 minutes. Service measurement compares that clock with Datum samples, and the public formatter adds a UTC suffix to naive timestamps.

Effect during response. Fresh samples can appear old. Operators and customers can see shifted incident or maintenance times.

Evidence. core/setup.py:17; core/service/measurement.py:163; frontend/src/api.js:16. The local System Settings and both clocks were read directly.

Proposed correction. Enforce one UTC clock contract. Serialize timestamps with an explicit UTC offset. Show the chosen time zone in maintenance forms and confirmation dialogs. Repair the local development fixture separately after checking its timestamp history.

Acceptance check. A known UTC sample stays fresh; the same incident instant agrees in Desk and public views. A scheduled window starts at the displayed time. Do not bulk-shift existing rows without inspection.

P1 · F02 Publish discards affected services

Current behavior. The Desk Publish dialog sends visibility and message. The controller assigns affected_services or an empty list, even when the operator already entered impact rows.

Effect during response. An incident can be published with no service impact. Its public status may not reflect the operator's intended scope.

Evidence. beacon/doctype/beacon_incident/beacon_incident.js:76; beacon/doctype/beacon_incident/beacon_incident.py:129. Source contract traced; no incident was published during review.

Proposed correction. Keep existing impact rows when the argument is absent. Put service, region and impact rows in the publication preview. Distinguish an omitted table from an explicit empty table. Permit a deliberate information-only notice with an explicit explanation.

Acceptance check. Existing impact rows survive Publish. New rows and intentionally empty notices behave as shown in the preview. Unlisted incidents stay out of the public index.

P1 · F03 The incident has no working evidence panels

Current behavior. Five HTML fields have no renderer: incident alerts and notifications, rule evaluations, resource current state, and silence matching alerts.

Effect during response. The responder must leave the incident to find its alerts, paging result or affected resource. Silence cannot show its scope before Save.

Evidence. alerts_html, notifications_html, evaluations_html, now_html and matching_html occur only in metadata or annotations. Blank incident, resource and silence panels were confirmed in the browser.

Proposed correction. Populate these views with permission-aware native related lists or small form sections. Each row must link to the relevant document. Show empty, loading, failure and stale states explicitly.

Acceptance check. An incident exposes its linked alerts and latest notification result on the first tab. A new silence previews matching resources and alerts before Save. Failed panel reads do not look like zero matches.

P1 · F04 Public health can ignore a declared impact

Current behavior. When an SLI state is Unknown, _get_service returns Unknown before applying a published incident's declared impact. Global-scope services are also excluded by the Region-only loop.

Effect during response. A declared outage can be obscured by missing telemetry. A supported Global service can be missing from the public page.

Evidence. core/status_page.py:67 and :244. Source-level findings; no public service state was changed during review.

Proposed correction. Display measurement availability separately from an explicit published impact. Include visible Global services in a distinct public group and in the appropriate summary. Preserve the accepted rule that SLI health can worsen without a published incident.

Acceptance check. A published Major Outage remains visible when measurements are Unknown. A Global service appears once. Internal and unlisted impact rows do not leak.

P2 · F05 Refresh time is presented as health freshness

Current behavior. The public updated_at value is the response-build time. Service checked_at and last_counted_minute are not returned. Desk overview has no prominent stale-evaluation or paging-paused summary.

Effect during response. A stopped scheduler can leave old health looking current. Zero firing alerts can be mistaken for healthy monitoring.

Evidence. core/status_page.py:81 and :234; Beacon Workspace definitions; Beacon Settings Safety tab. Live public page displayed a recent Updated label alongside Unknown service states.

Proposed correction. Show last measured time and missing coverage independently from response refresh time. Put paused paging, failed sources and stale or broken evaluations near the top of the operator view. Derive this from existing records; do not create duplicate state.

Acceptance check. With measurement stopped, freshness becomes visibly stale while the page remains reachable. Unknown stays distinct from Operational. Agree the freshness threshold before implementation.

P2 · F06 Frequent actions take unnecessary steps

Current behavior. Acknowledge, Test and Check Connection share the same confirmation helper as risky actions. Results render as raw JSON. Important actions are all peers in one Actions menu.

Effect during response. Operators confirm safe reads and scan implementation output when they need a quick answer.

Evidence. public/js/beacon.bundle.js:4; beacon_alert_rule.js; beacon_region.js; beacon_service.js.

Proposed correction. Keep the established Actions group, but surface the current primary task. Run safe reads directly. Show result tables with region, success or error, duration, row count and a compact sample. Keep destructive actions separated and confirmed.

Acceptance check. Acknowledge takes one deliberate click. Test has no redundant confirmation. Publish, held removals, monitoring disable and phone calls still state their effects before submission.

P2 · F07 Incident context is split across tabs

Current behavior. The current tab order is Overview, Impact, Updates, Paging, RCA. Acknowledgement, current escalation, latest update and customer impact are not combined at the point of response.

Effect during response. The responder changes tabs to learn who is acting, whether a page was delivered, and what customers can see.

Evidence. beacon/doctype/beacon_incident/beacon_incident.json field_order and the live form.

Proposed correction. Keep a compact form-dashboard summary above tabs. The first tab shows alerts, scope, current response and latest update. Use Overview, Updates, Customer impact, Response and RCA. Keep RCA last and internal fields collapsed.

Acceptance check. At phone width, the responder can identify status, severity, acknowledgement, active alerts, paging state and the next action without visiting another tab.

P2 · F08 Suppression and held removals lack a clear impact review

Current behavior. Silence has no live preview. The held-removal action shows only the first 20 resource IDs as JSON and does not state that the list is truncated.

Effect during response. A responder may silence too much or approve a resource removal without understanding descendants and monitoring coverage.

Evidence. beacon_silence.json matching_html; beacon_resource_source.py:82; core/resource/source.py:164. Blank silence preview was confirmed live.

Proposed correction. Show matching count, sample, descendant scope, full filtered-list link, reason and expiry. Show held removals with total count, the truncation label, source freshness and the affected monitoring scope. Keep the existing no-empty-match and 30-day silence guards.

Acceptance check. A region-wide match is clearly broad. Preview and application use the same match logic. A 21-item removal displays 21 total, a labeled sample and access to the full set.

P2 · F09 Some read helpers use the acknowledgement guard

Current behavior. get_private_link and get_affected_resources call _check_staff. On a Triggered incident that guard calls _acknowledge, which updates Call notification rows. Neither read helper persists the incident's acknowledgement state itself.

Effect during response. A read invoked through a committing document-method request can change notification state without a consistent incident acknowledgement.

Evidence. beacon_incident.py:165, :201, :381 and :345; Frappe handler.run_doc_method. Source-level trace; the mutating path was not exercised live.

Proposed correction. Separate permission checks from acknowledgement. Read helpers must be side-effect free. Keep acknowledgement and its incident plus notification writes together in an action.

Acceptance check. Reading scope or copying a private link changes no incident or notification rows. Explicit Acknowledge updates both consistently.

P2 · F10 Form affordances disagree with backend ownership

Current behavior. File-loaded rule fields look editable although Save refuses changes other than Enabled. The Incident Type selector remains editable after creation. Settings shows administrator-only actions to read-only responders. A test page permits a blank user for Call or Email. The responder Email field is not used by EmailNotifier, which reads User.email.

Effect during response. The operator discovers restrictions only after work, can attempt the wrong state flow, or misunderstands the effective notification address.

Evidence. beacon_alert_rule.py:47; beacon_incident.json type; beacon_settings.js:24; beacon_settings.py:98; core/incident/channels.py:57. Metadata, permissions and action methods were inspected.

Proposed correction. Make source-managed fields visibly read-only, lock incident type after creation, and match action visibility to permissions. Require an active recipient for Call or Email and display the effective address. Keep backend checks authoritative.

Acceptance check. A responder sees only allowed actions. File-owned values cannot appear locally editable. Phone and email tests cannot submit without a valid recipient. No duplicate email source remains unexplained.

P2 · F11 Lists and drill-down links are not tuned for triage

Current behavior. Lists rely on many in_list_view flags and default modified or creation sorting. Resources lead with source identifiers and internal hashes. Service configuration has no first-screen link to its current health records. Suppression reasons are plain text.

Effect during response. The most urgent record and its next diagnostic page take extra scanning or navigation.

Evidence. All main DocType field_order, in_list_view and sort settings; no app ListView settings files. Live forms confirmed the visible fields.

Proposed correction. Use explicit per-list columns and filters. Prefer active or failed records in dedicated shortcuts, then severity and age where supported. Put status, reason and freshness before identifiers. Link incident, alert, rule, resource, evaluation, silence and source in both directions.

Acceptance check. A five-record mixed-status drill exposes the urgent record first. Each failing record reaches its rule, source or incident without a manual global search. Preserve history views.

P2 · F12 Public detail navigation and history need clearer behavior

Current behavior. useCall loads on mount and a timer, but does not watch route parameters. History and detail do not set the site title. Uptime bars are spans with title-only details. The aggregate uptime period and coverage are not labeled in the service row.

Effect during response. Client-side incident navigation can keep an old detail until the next poll. Phone and keyboard users cannot reliably inspect daily uptime. A short sample can look like a full 90-day measurement.

Evidence. frontend/src/api.js:21; pages/IncidentPage.vue:53; HistoryPage.vue; components/UptimeBars.vue. Live public page has 270 bars and zero focusable day controls.

Proposed correction. Reload and clear obsolete results when route identity changes, and guard response ordering. Use a shared site title, explicit time-zone labels, a period label and coverage details. Make day details tappable and keyboard-accessible. Keep public errors generic and private internals hidden.

Acceptance check. Moving between two public incidents immediately requests the correct record. An invalid or expired private link shows a safe state. A day detail works by tap, Enter and Escape. Partial coverage is visible.

Proposed hierarchy for every page

All internal work stays in native Desk. “Primary task” means the useful next action for the shown state. Existing actions remain in the Actions group; an optional prominent shortcut must call that same controller method. Do not duplicate business logic in the form script. Do not make state-machine fields editable.

Operator flows

These flows describe the intended response after the proposed corrections. They are not a claim that each action was exercised in the local review.

1. Respond to an automatic incident

Start: A notification opens an incident.

  1. Read status, severity, scope, acknowledgement and measurement freshness.
  2. Acknowledge once if the incident is Triggered. Confirm that incident and notification state agree.
  3. Inspect linked alerts, their evidence and their runbooks. Follow parent-resource context before treating every symptom independently.
  4. Mitigate the infrastructure through its owning tool. Beacon records and coordinates; it does not repair hosts.
  5. Post an internal update or use the explicit publication flow for customer communication.
  6. Wait for current evaluations and service measurements. Resolve only when recovery is real or an intentional, scoped silence is confirmed.

Done: The response has one visible acknowledgement, current evidence, a working update record and a clear next action.

2. Publish customer impact

Start: Customers need an explanation or a planned notice.

  1. Select service, region and impact. Blank region must visibly mean all regions.
  2. Preview the title, visibility, customer message and impact rows together.
  3. Choose Public or Unlisted deliberately. Preserve existing impact rows unless explicitly changed.
  4. Publish through the Incident document method. Keep internal notes out of the customer message.
  5. Open the public preview or copy the private link. Confirm that the intended service and region are affected.

Done: The customer view matches the submitted scope. Unknown telemetry does not erase an explicitly declared outage.

3. Schedule maintenance

Start: An operator plans work on resources.

  1. Create a Maintenance incident. Enter start, end and time zone before the scope.
  2. Select the region, resources and descendant behavior. Review the expanded scope.
  3. Select the affected services and explain whether burn alerts are muted.
  4. Publish before the start if public maintenance exclusion is intended. Show the actual eligibility rather than an editable promise.
  5. At the start, verify In Progress, suppression and the displayed remaining window. Extend deliberately when work runs late.
  6. Complete the window and observe the existing post-maintenance suppression grace. Recheck measurements and alerts after it.

Done: The declared window, suppression scope and actual SLO exclusion agree. Completing maintenance is distinct from resolving an incident.

4. Silence one problem

Start: A known problem should stop paging temporarily.

  1. Start from the incident, alert or resource so known match fields are prefilled.
  2. Enter the reason and expiry. Keep the UTC or local-time choice explicit.
  3. Inspect the match count, sample and descendant scope. A preview failure blocks confirmation.
  4. Save with the backend's existing match and expiry checks.
  5. Confirm the suppressed reason on the alert. Extend or revoke from the same record when necessary.

Done: The operator can state what is muted, why, until when and how to undo it. The alert remains distinguishable from recovery.

5. Diagnose a rule that stopped checking

Start: A rule evaluation is Erroring, Broken or stale.

  1. Open the failed evaluation from the rule or region summary.
  2. Read last success, last attempt, error, duration and unknown-resource count.
  3. Follow the rule and run its safe Test action. Use a structured per-region result.
  4. For a file-owned rule, change the source file through the normal engineering workflow; do not imply Desk can edit it.
  5. Run Now when permitted, then refresh the persisted result. A queued request is not a successful evaluation.

Done: The check returns to Healthy with a new successful run time. A query error never resolves an existing alert.

6. Review source removals

Start: A resource fetch fails or holds a large removal.

  1. Read last success, last attempt, error and the held-removal count before editing credentials.
  2. Compare the source response with the affected resources and descendants.
  3. Use the full filtered list when the preview is truncated. Inspect conflicting and orphaned telemetry IDs separately.
  4. Fetch again after the upstream cause is understood. A failed fetch keeps the existing resource list.
  5. Apply held removals only after an explicit scope review and confirmation. Check monitoring coverage afterward.

Done: A transient or broken source does not silently remove resources or hide an active incident.

7. Diagnose Unknown service health

Start: A service is Unknown or its latest measurement is old.

  1. Check Service State freshness and valid-event coverage first.
  2. Inspect the region probe and resource source. Check the UTC clock contract before assuming samples are stale.
  3. Open recent Service Minutes and the SLI Test result. Look for zero valid events, missing resource IDs or query failures.
  4. Check public maintenance exclusion and cause alerts before interpreting silent reporters.
  5. Verify a new measurement and its transition hold. Do not relabel Unknown as Operational to clear the screen.

Done: The operator can distinguish unavailable measurement from a measured outage or a pending healthy transition.

8. Diagnose a missed page

Start: A responder did not receive or acknowledge an expected notification.

  1. Read the incident's escalation state, next page time and latest notification outcome.
  2. Inspect the effective on-call order, override expiry and paging pause.
  3. Open the notification error and its channel configuration. Display the actual recipient source.
  4. If allowed, send an explicit test to a valid active recipient. Explain that a Call can contact a real phone and incur a charge.
  5. Verify delivery and acknowledgement separately. Do not add an automatic resend policy as a UI shortcut.

Done: The delivery failure and next escalation are visible without guessing from a Queued or Sent label.

9. Resolve, hand over and write the RCA

Start: Mitigation is complete or the response changes hands.

  1. Review unsuppressed firing alerts, latest measurements and the public message.
  2. If alerts still fire, leave the incident open or explicitly choose a scoped silence with an expiry and reason.
  3. Resolve with a message and deliberate audience. Keep customer publication separate from internal notes.
  4. Record the root cause, mitigation and follow-up in the internal RCA. Publish only a reviewed public RCA.
  5. For a handover, include current state, actions already taken, outstanding risk and the next check.

Done: A resolved incident is not merely acknowledged or muted. Internal RCA and customer RCA stay separate.

10. Inspect public status and history

Start: A customer or responder opens public status, a public incident, or an unlisted link.

  1. Read current service impact, region, measurement availability and last measured time.
  2. Open the incident timeline and inspect the latest customer-facing update.
  3. Inspect a day through a tap or keyboard action, including uptime period and observed coverage.
  4. Navigate to another incident and confirm that its content changes immediately.
  5. Use a safe not-found state for unpublished, invalid and expired private links. Keep resource details, recipients, tokens and internal notes out of public output.

Done: Public status is readable on a phone, clearly labels missing data and does not expose operational internals.

Desk text and actions

  • All Desk text uses ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English: short sentences, active voice and simple words.
  • A confirmation shows the question first. The effect follows in a separate paragraph.
  • One word for one thing: "notify" for on-call messages and calls, "held removals" for removals that wait for approval, "child resources" for the resources under a resource.
  • Actions follow the order of use. Frequent actions come first. Actions that are hard to undo come last: Merge Into, Cancel Maintenance and Regenerate Private Link.
  • Read-only views, such as Affected Resources, are in the Related group.

Other fix found during the checks

A removed resource stayed Active forever. The first fetch counted the resource as missing. The next fetches got "not modified" from the source, so Beacon did not count the second miss. Now Beacon keeps no ETag while a removal waits, so the next fetch reads the full list.

Boundary and concurrency checks
